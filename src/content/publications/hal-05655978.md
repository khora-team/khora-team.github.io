Software energy consumption is often intangible, as users interact with systems through interfaces (e.g., keyboard, mouse, screen) that are physically and perceptually detached from the underlying hardware. Increasingly powerful and integrated computing devices further obscure both their energy usage and the physical feedback (e.g., heat or sound) associated with software execution.Most eco-feedback displays rely solely on visual feedback to convey information about resource consumption [1], rarely providing direct haptic feedback about software energy use.To address this gap, the Multimodal Output for Understanding Software Energy (MOUSE) system introduces haptic and pseudohaptic feedback mechanisms to make software energy consumption more perceptible.
